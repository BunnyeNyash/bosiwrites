Hey everyone! 👋

**Fintech** sits at the intersection of two things I’ve come to love: *finance* and *technology*, and today I want to share why for this love. It’s been a winding path that’s brought me here, but one I’m truly grateful for.

When I first joined the University to study commerce, I wasn’t exactly thrilled. Honestly, I didn’t even want to study anything business-related. It felt... Boring, and just didn’t feel exciting to me at the time.. But life is funny like that. It nudges you toward paths you never thought you’d take

When the time came to choose a specialization in commerce, I was all over the place. At first, I thought, “*Maybe marketing?*” But after some thought, I realized it wasn’t quite for me. Then I considered accounting, thinking it might be a good fit, but again, it didn’t click. I imagined myself doing it long-term and was like, “*Naaaaaah, not for me*.” Finally, I landed on finance. At first, it was just the least bad option, but little did I know it would eventually become something I’m deeply passionate about.

Then came an unexpected twist. A friend of mine, who was studying computer science at the same university, introduced me to software engineering, and for the first time, I felt this spark. Technology felt like a whole new world where I could create things, solve problems, and at the same time, innovate to make people's lives better. I loved it!  I started learning through the rigorous ALX Software Engineering program and got really into it, but as life got busier, I couldn’t keep up with learning. Between classes, assignments, and everything else, I eventually had to stop. Without consistent practice, I gradually forgot most of what I had learned, which was frustrating but also a lesson for me.

Fast-forward to my final year at Kirinyaga University, where I worked on a project titled “*A Case Study on the Financial Literacy Levels Among Male and Female Students at Kirinyaga University*.”, under the supervision of Dr. Patrick Mwangi. At first, the project felt like a task that I didn't want to get involved with, but My supervisor's motivation and "I want perfection from you" behaviour made me feel "one" with it. All that research he made me do completely changed how I saw finance. The findings from the project were literally shocking as more than half of the students, even though they were at an age where they had to manage their finances, were considered financially illiterate. It hit me how many people, especially students, struggle to understand money and make good financial decisions. The project made me realize something important: there’s a serious gap in financial literacy, but with the right tools and innovative solutions, we can help bridge it.

And that’s where my love for fintech was born. It felt like everything clicked. My love for finance, my interest in tech, and my desire to make a difference.

Fintech excites me because it’s about more than just numbers and coding. It's even more than just an industry to me.  It’s a way to:

* **Improving Financial Inclusion:** Giving people access to banking, payments, and investment tools they never had before, ensuring no one is left behind.
*  **Driving Innovation:** Using technology to create smarter, simpler solutions that make managing money easier for everyone.
*  **Empowering Others:** Developing tools that help people understand their finances better and make confident financial decisions.
*  **Simplifying Finance:** Breaking down complex financial concepts into accessible, user-friendly solutions for everyday life.

So, here I am, at the start of this journey. I’m learning, experimenting, and figuring out how I can contribute to this exciting field. It’s not perfect, and I’m sure I’ll stumble along the way, but I’m passionate about where this path can take me.

If you’re passionate about fintech, are on a similar journey, or have advice to share, I’d love to connect and learn from your experiences. Let’s learn, grow, and innovate together! 🚀
